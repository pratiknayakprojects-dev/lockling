Where to store ID scans and passwords offline on iPhone

Passport scans, ID numbers, bank cards and recovery codes are the things you least want sitting in your camera roll. Here's how the common options compare if you'd rather keep them off the cloud.

Apple's Passwords app

Good for website and app logins, with autofill. It syncs through iCloud Keychain when that's turned on, so it isn't offline by design. It isn't built for ID scans, card photos or documents.

Locked notes in Notes

You can lock a note with your passcode or Face ID. Notes in an iCloud account sync to iCloud. To keep them on the device, use the "On My iPhone" account (turn it on in Settings → Notes). Notes have no fields for card numbers or expiry dates and no reminders.

Your camera roll

The easiest and the riskiest. Anyone scrolling your photos can see them, and they sync with iCloud Photos.

An offline vault

Lockling keeps passwords, API and encryption keys, bank cards, IDs, Wi-Fi details, licences and secure notes in one encrypted place on your iPhone:

  • Scan to fill. Scan an ID or card and Lockling fills in the number, name and dates. The scan is read on your iPhone with Apple's on-device text recognition.
  • Expiry reminders. Get a reminder before an ID or card expires. The notification never names the item.
  • Copy safely. Copied values stay on this iPhone and clear from the clipboard after 60 seconds.
  • Attach the document. Keep the scan or photo with the record, encrypted with AES-256.
